# Search boundary

Decide the limits of a literature map before you screen or cite. Record the decisions in the search-record template. Leave a field empty until you have decided it. Query text stays in public wording: do not place unpublished manuscript sentences or restricted values into a query.

## Question

Name the system or population, the comparison, and the outcome the active paper needs from prior work. If any of the three is missing, this is idea critique: stop and do not search.

A broad question becomes separate targets, such as a method, a confound, and a prior magnitude. Give each target its own boundary and its own record. Do not pool the hits and then describe them as one search.

## Window

Choose a start date and an end date before you look at how many hits return. The end date is the access date unless you declare an earlier cutoff. Say why the window starts there: a method appeared, a measurement changed, or the paper stated a look-back. An omitted start date is a known gap, not an open-ended search.

## Sources

Name each source family you will use: a scholarly index, a preprint server when the field circulates work early, and the reference lists of seed papers you already trust for this question. A coverage claim needs more than one family. Every family you skip is a known gap.

When you already hold a stable identifier, open that record before any broad query. Only then borrow its title words for the query, and only after the opened record is the work you meant.

## Query

Write the query, the synonyms you included, and the terms you excluded. Run a pilot against one paper you already know should be included.

- If that paper is missing, add a synonym or a source and record the new query with a new access date.
- If most hits fall outside the question, add a limiting concept and record that change the same way.

Each revision is its own line. Do not overwrite the query that produced an earlier count.

## Inclusion rule

Write the rule before screening. State which designs, languages, and publication states count: peer-reviewed article, preprint, review, or another state you name. Apply the rule to every hit. A favored paper does not get a private exception. When you exclude a hit, record the reason, not only a tally.

## Stop rule

Pick one rule before you interpret a gap:

- A fixed cap: a stated number of hits per source, then stop.
- Saturation: one further round of terms, or one further source, adds no newly included work.
- Seed plus chain: a named set of seed identifiers, then one pass over works that cite them and one pass over works they cite, then stop.

When the rule is met, stop. Reading past it is a new search and needs a new record. Do not extend the boundary because another paper would make the gap look cleaner.

## Known gaps

List skipped languages, skipped sources, full texts you could not open, and dates you did not cover. These lines stay attached to the evidence map. They are why an empty result is "not found in this search."

After the record is filled, check any source you will rely on in [Citation check](citation-check.md).
