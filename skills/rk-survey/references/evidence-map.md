# Evidence map

Write the map only after a search boundary is recorded and each source you rely on has a citation check. Arrange sources by what they did and what they measured. The order of a hit list is not a structure.

## Group by design and outcome

Place each included source in a design class: experiment, observational study, simulation or benchmark, method or tool paper, or review. Inside a class, group by the outcome the source measured. A source may belong to more than one outcome; say when it does.

For each group, state:

- the design those sources share, and the assumption that lets their outcomes be compared
- where the results agree
- where they conflict
- any mismatch that blocks a comparison: population, measure, noise regime, data split, or reported uncertainty

If you set two numbers side by side, either name that mismatch or state that the definitions match. An unmatched pair is not one effect.

## Result, speculation, and paraphrase

Keep three layers separate:

- Result: a finding the source reports from its own design. Name whether you took it from the full text or from the abstract.
- Speculation: a mechanism, implication, or next study the source offers without a result for it.
- Paraphrase: a later review's restatement. Cite the review for the restatement and the original source for the result. For a load-bearing claim, the review does not replace the original.

An abstract-only citation check caps the result layer at that abstract.

## Novelty limit of the search

Tie the limit to the search record in one place:

- the question, access date, queries, inclusion rule, and stop rule
- the nearest included work, by identifier
- what that work already established
- the known gaps: sources, languages, dates, and closed full texts

An absence inside that boundary is "Not found in this search." It is not "never studied." Do not write "first" or "no prior work" unless the recorded boundary and the nearest-work check support a narrower claim, and name that boundary beside the claim.

A sibling draft, bibliography, figure, codebase, or unpublished result is not a row in this map.

## Systematic-review label

Use a systematic-review label only if a protocol was followed: written before screening, and visible in the search record as the inclusion rule and the stop rule you actually used. Otherwise the product is a literature map or a gap statement. Searching several sources without that protocol does not earn the label. Do not claim a reporting standard you did not carry out.

## What to hand back

Return a short map: the search boundary, the closest work, the conflicting work, what each checked source establishes, the gap that remains inside the boundary, and the citation-check status of every load-bearing source. Method choice, data analysis, and manuscript prose wait for their own skills.
