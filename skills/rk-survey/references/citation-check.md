# Citation check

Apply this to each source that will support a claim, a baseline, or a gap. Use one citation checklist for that source. A search snippet is not a completed check. Provenance for the source is the access date, the source name, the identifier, whether you opened the full text or an abstract, and the retraction or correction status.

## Identifier

Record a stable identifier and open it. Prefer a DOI. If the work has none, record a preprint identifier, a publisher identifier, or a stable address on the venue. The page you open must be the work whose title you will cite. If it is a different work, stop and find the right record. If no identifier opens, do not cite the source.

A reference suggested from memory stays unchecked until this step succeeds. Do not fill a missing identifier by guessing.

## Version

Record the version you opened: preprint, accepted manuscript, or published version, plus any version number or date printed on the work. If a later version exists and you have not opened it, either open it and cite that version, or cite the version you opened and state that a later version exists unchecked. Do not describe the later version from the earlier one.

A preprint and a later journal article of the same work are one source. Keep the version you rely on and point the other record at it. Label a superseded version as superseded.

## Venue

Take the venue name from the work or from its version-of-record page. A shortened label on a results list is not enough when the claim depends on where the work appeared. For a conference paper, record the proceedings name. For a preprint, record the repository. For a chapter, record the book and the publisher.

## Retraction or correction

On the opened record, look for a retraction, a withdrawal, an expression of concern, or a correction tied to that identifier. Write the status you found, including "none found on this record," and the access date.

- Retracted or withdrawn: do not use the work as support. You may note the retraction when the history of the work is itself the point.
- Expression of concern: do not treat the findings as settled. Say that the concern is present.
- Correction: cite the corrected statement. If you only saw the uncorrected text, say so, and do not quote figures the correction replaced.

If an early online date and an issue date disagree, record which date you are citing.

## Abstract only

If you opened only an abstract, a snippet, or a title, mark the source "abstract only." That mark may decide inclusion under the inclusion rule. It does not allow you to:

- report a number, interval, or sample size that the abstract itself does not state
- describe a method step the abstract does not state
- assign a limitation, dataset, or split that lives only in a body you did not open
- supply volume, issue, or pages you did not see

When a precise claim needs the body, open the full text, change the mark to "full text," and record the access date. Until that change, the evidence map carries the abstract-only limit next to the source.

## Same work twice

The same identifier twice is one source. A similar title with a different identifier is a different source until you show it is the same work. Check title, authors, and year against the opened record before you treat them as confirmed.

Then group the checked sources in [Evidence map](evidence-map.md). The boundary those sources belong to is [Search boundary](search-boundary.md).
