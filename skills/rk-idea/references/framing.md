# Framing

Use this when the open decision is pursue, reframe, defer, or reject. The output is a question and a labeled judgment, not a score.

## Problem versus proposal

Write two separate statements before judging:

- Problem: what is unknown, inconsistent, or failing, in which system, under which regime.
- Proposal: the fix, comparison, model, or argument being offered.

When those statements describe the same act, the proposal has replaced the problem. Split them and judge again. A renamed benchmark, a new configuration, or a new label is a proposal. It becomes a contribution only after a later bounded search says what nearby work already did. Until then the contribution is provisional and novelty stays unverified.

## What the question names

A question that can be pursued names:

- the system or population
- the unit of analysis
- the outcome
- the comparison, when the claim is comparative
- the regime, timeframe, or horizon
- the decision or understanding the answer would change

If the request bundles independent questions, split them. Each question gets its own judgment.

Optional frames, and only when the question is of that kind:

- Intervention: population, intervention, comparator, outcome, horizon.
- Exposure: population, exposure, comparator, outcome.
- Diagnostic: population, index test, reference standard, target condition.
- Prognostic: population, factor, outcome, time.
- Theoretical or formal: the statement, the context, and what would count as an answer.

An intervention frame is not a default for a mechanistic, formal, or descriptive question.

## Claim type

Set one primary type, the type the cheapest honest check can speak to:

- Descriptive: what the pattern is, in a defined scope.
- Associational: how measured variables co-vary.
- Predictive: what would be forecast in a named setting, on observations not used to invent the pattern.
- Causal: what would differ under a stated intervention or exposure contrast.
- Mechanistic: through which process that difference would occur.
- Formal: what follows from stated assumptions, or which counterexample defeats them.

A theorem, a simulation, and an empirical contrast are different evidence. If the wording says the proposal causes the outcome and the available check is a co-occurrence, a fit, or a story, the claim type is associational, predictive, or still unframed.

## At most three framings

When more than one precise question is serious, compare at most three. For each, record the contribution, the evidence burden, the cost, and the way it fails first. Recommend one. Leave the others named. Do not merge them into a wider claim that no single check can answer.

## Feasibility is its own question

When it is unknown whether the work can run, state a feasibility question with a measurable threshold. Size, memory, time, iteration count, and convergence answer that question only. Keep the scientific question in its own sentence. A feasibility measurement never supports the scientific claim. It also never blocks recording the scientific question.

If the scientific design depends on the threshold, defer the scientific judgment until the threshold is measured, and say so. The feasibility measurement itself may proceed.

## Judgment

Walk this sequence once.

1. Reframe when the problem and the proposal are tangled, several questions are bundled, the unit or regime is wrong, or the claim type is stronger than any check on the table.
2. Reject when, after that split, no result would count against the proposal under its own assumptions, no check can bear on the claim type, or an ethics, safety, privacy, or regulatory block rules out the work and no narrower question avoids the block. Also reject when the burden is out of proportion to the decision the answer would change and no narrower question is worth asking. State which of these fired.
3. Defer when a named missing fact still blocks the scientific question: access, an unfinished required review, a measurement that does not yet exist, or a bounded search that rk-survey has not recorded. Write the fact. Deferral keeps the problem open.
4. Pursue only when the question names the items above, the claim type matches the check, a rival and a weakening result are on the record, and none of the earlier branches fired. Pursue hands the question to protocol design. It does not lock a protocol and does not establish the claim.

One encouraging pilot, a familiar narrative, or a clean diagram does not meet pursue.

If the contribution depends on being first, defer that part until rk-survey records a bounded search. Unverified novelty on its own neither pursues nor rejects the question.

## Last pass

Before returning the judgment, confirm:

- every load-bearing construct is named
- the unit of analysis is unambiguous
- the scope is one question
- a result that would weaken the candidate is either recorded or the judgment is not pursue
