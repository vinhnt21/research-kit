# Biases and fallacies

Use this only when a recognition check would change pursue, reframe, defer, or reject. If none would, say so and stop. Do not recite a catalog.

## Order

Name, in this order: the bias, the evidence limit, and the fallacy. Then restate the claim those three affect. Write "none" in a slot that does not apply. The claim comes last so the label does not replace the judgment.

## Preferred story

Confirmation, anchoring on the first account, or defending a candidate after a contrary result: the check can only come out in favor of the favorite, or every miss gets an extra clause. Evidence limit: no result on the table would weaken it. Fallacy when present: special pleading, moving the standard once it is met, or redefining the case so the counterexample no longer counts. Judgment: reframe until a weakening result exists, or reject.

## After the fact

Hindsight, or a hypothesis written after the outcome was seen: the prediction is being backdated. Evidence limit: the target result was already visible, so the analysis is exploratory. Fallacy when present: drawing the target around a cluster that was already found, or switching the outcome to the one that matched. Judgment: do not pursue as confirmatory. Reframe as an exploratory candidate, or defer a new check on observations not yet used.

## Selected reports

Publication bias, citation of favorable reports, or availability of a dramatic case: the file that is easy to recall is being treated as the literature. Evidence limit: the slice is the positive or memorable part, and there is no bounded search. Fallacy when present: suppressing the misses, or treating a vivid instance as the rate. Judgment: defer any claim that the literature supports the idea. Novelty stays unverified.

## Substitutes for a check

Bandwagon, deference to a title, preference for the new or the traditional, or loyalty to an outsider story: the reason to proceed is who said it, how recent it is, or how old it is. A conflict of interest is a reason to inspect the check, not a proof that the result is false. Evidence limit: authority, fashion, age, or origin. Fallacy when present: appeal to authority, novelty, tradition, the speaker, emotion, desirable consequences, "natural," or what ought to be true. Judgment: those reasons do not pursue. Require a discriminating check, or reject.

## Who was measured

Selection, attrition, survivorship, volunteer entry, or a sample restricted to an extreme spectrum: the pattern may be who remains. Evidence limit: the analyzed set is not the stated population. Fallacy when present: reading a group rate as an individual's result, or the reverse. Judgment: add that rival, or reframe the population. Do not pursue a causal claim on this pattern alone.

## The measurement makes the pattern

Observer expectation, unequal handling of groups, recall, instrument drift, or a response shaped by what the respondent thinks is wanted: the recorded outcome can move without the construct moving. Lead time and length time apply when earlier or slower detection is being read as a better outcome. Evidence limit: the instrument or the detection process is unseparated from the effect. Fallacy when present: treating the recording as the construct. Judgment: the discriminating check targets the measurement, or defer.

## Cause read into an association

A common cause, a reversed direction, or a single-cause story: order or co-variation is offered as the mechanism. Evidence limit: no intervention contrast and no estimand. Fallacy when present: after-this-therefore-because-of-this, correlation as cause, or one cause for a many-cause outcome. Judgment: keep the claim associational, or reframe until the estimand and a discriminating check exist.

## Base rate and extreme values

An extreme baseline, a rare event, or a low prior treated as if the hit were the rate: the next observation is expected to be less extreme, or the prior makes the hit weak. Evidence limit: one extreme draw, or a base rate left out. Fallacy when present: base-rate neglect, or swapping the probability of the data given the story with the probability of the story given the data. Judgment: reframe the contrast to include a control or the prior, or defer. Do not pursue on the extreme draw alone.

A sign that flips when groups are pooled is the same kind of warning. Name the unit before pursuing. The pooled pattern and the within-group pattern are different claims.

## Logic that seals the idea

The favorite is declared true because its predicted pattern appeared, or the only alternatives offered are two cartoons. Evidence limit: other accounts predict the same pattern, or the rival was restated into something easier to dismiss. Fallacy: affirming the consequent, a false pair of options, circular definition, a term used in two senses, or an untestable gap-filler. Judgment: keep the mixed and unknown path. Reframe or reject if no observation could count against the claim.

Absence inside an unfinished search is not novelty and is not proof the claim is false. Evidence limit: no bounded search, or a search that could not have seen a counterexample. Fallacy: argument from ignorance. Judgment: defer the novelty claim. Do not reject a rival merely because this pass did not find it.

Rejecting a partial check because it is imperfect, or pursuing because a speculative chain ends somewhere extreme, does not change the evidence. Compare the check with the live rival, not with a perfect study or an imagined endpoint.

## Statistical readings that would settle the idea too early

These misreadings do not pursue and do not reject:

- a tail probability treated as the probability the candidate is true
- a result on the familiar side of a threshold treated as important, and a result just on the other side treated as none
- a non-significant result treated as absence
- many looks, subgroups, or outcomes, with one hit offered as confirmatory
- a tiny or extreme sample treated as a stable effect
- power computed from the observed result treated as new information
- a fit on the same observations treated as a prediction
- a forecast past the observed regime treated as support
- a non-significant covariate treated as proof that confounding is gone
- dropping incomplete rows when missingness can depend on the outcome

Evidence limit: the number does not mean what the proposal needs it to mean. Fallacy when present: multiple unplanned comparisons, or a target chosen after the hits. Judgment: reframe the decision rule. The analysis procedure itself belongs to rk-data.

Counting only what is easy to quantify, while the construct stays unmeasured, is a reason to reframe the operationalization.

## Evidence strength against the claim type

Match the claim to evidence that could carry it. The design label does not pursue the idea.

- A causal or intervention claim needs a contrast that could identify it, with the open assumptions named. An association, a score, or a story does not carry it.
- A predictive claim needs observations not used to invent the pattern.
- A mechanistic claim needs a prediction a rival process would not make.
- A formal claim needs the proof obligation or a counterexample. A ladder of study types does not apply.
- A descriptive claim needs a population, a measurement, and a scope. A causal verb does not upgrade it.
- A synthesis supports only the studies and comparisons it included. A selective or heterogeneous pool is an evidence limit: defer.
- A case report, an anecdote, or an expert assertion can motivate a question. It does not pursue a general or causal claim.
- A large or repeated observational pattern can be pursued only as an assumption-dependent causal candidate, and only with the estimand and the discriminating check named. Otherwise reframe to association.
- A strong design aimed at another population, comparator, or outcome does not answer this question. Reframe or defer.

A converged set of independent checks can support pursue inside its scope. One study, whatever its label, does not close the rivals it did not test.
