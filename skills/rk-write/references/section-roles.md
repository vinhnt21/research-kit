# Section jobs

These are jobs, not a required outline. IMRaD is an optional arrangement for some original-research reports. A proof, a methods paper, a theory note, a combined results and discussion, or another form is right when the work calls for it.

When a venue is in scope, that venue's current instructions win over every job below. If the venue omits a section named here, leave it out. If the venue requires a section this guide does not name, follow the venue and still give each substantive sentence one opened source.

## Abstract

The job is to tell a reader who may stop here the question, the procedure that was used, the result that was obtained, and a conclusion no stronger than that result.

Write the abstract after the results text exists. Every number uses the same definition, unit, and analysis set as the results. Leave out a comparison, a mechanism, or a scope the results do not show. The venue chooses structured or prose form, length, and headings.

## Introduction

The job is to move from checked context to this paper's question.

State what the opened sources establish, what remains uncertain, and the gap this paper addresses. Close on the question, objective, or prespecified hypothesis. Announce a finding only when the results contain it. A claim that prior work is absent needs sources checked for this paper.

## Related work

The job is to show how the closest checked sources differ from this paper in question, assumption, procedure, or evidence.

Group by that difference. Include the strongest relevant alternatives when the paper draws a comparison. Each citation is a source opened for the sentence it supports. If those sources are not yet checked, leave the claims blank. Searching the literature is outside this skill.

## Method

The job is to report the procedure that was used, with enough detail for a reader to judge whether it can answer the question.

Cover the design, materials or data, comparators, measures, analysis, and any deviation that changes interpretation, as they apply to this study. Describe what the record says happened, including a step that was dropped. Ethics, registration, and availability statements use facts the human authors have already recorded. The venue's required headings for those statements win.

Running a new analysis is outside this skill. Describe the run that already exists.

## Results

The job is to report what was observed or estimated, in the order of the questions the paper declared.

Give units, denominators or sample sizes, and uncertainty when the result has them. Keep negative, null, and inconclusive findings that belong to the study record. Keep confirmatory, exploratory, and descriptive results distinct. State the result, then the interpretation, unless the venue combines those jobs.

A table or figure is optional. Use one when exact values or a pattern are clearer than a sentence, and only for a result already in the record. Prose, display, and abstract use the same values. A threshold crossing is not importance by itself. A non-significant result is not equivalence and is not evidence of no effect.

## Conclusion

The job is to answer the paper's question at the strength the results support, and to name a concrete limit.

Add no result, method, or scope beyond the results. A next step is a direction for later work. It is not an experiment already run. When the venue uses a discussion in place of a conclusion, or beside it, interpretation still starts from the reported result and stops at the same bound.

## Before handing the draft back

Read the abstract and the conclusion against the results. Narrow any sentence that states a result, a comparison, or a scope the results do not contain.
